02The operating model is published by the wrong company

The question Phase 2 exists to answer is whether a real transacting channel is structurally appropriate for Introw. The obvious place to look is the vendor's partner pages. That place is empty.

Measured across twelve public partner pages

At NetApp, Sophos, Cisco, Nutanix and Forcepoint — companies whose channels are unambiguously distributor-led — the words two-tier, through a distributor, authorised distributor, rebate, MDF and co-op appear zero times. Those pages are reseller-recruitment marketing. The operating model lives behind the partner portal.

It is published, though — by the distributors. A vendor on a distributor's carried-brands list sells through distribution by definition, and that list is public.

This is the strongest structural discriminator Phase 2 found, and it inverts where the evidence is looked for: not on the account, but on its counterparties.

It is a demotion, never an exclusion

Cubbit is a real Introw customer and is carried by Exclusive Networks. Distribution and a direct programme plainly coexist. In the model Cubbit is routed to research_required — sent to a human, not rejected.

Industrial: public-data limited

A reading that makes this coherent rather than merely disappointing

These companies came from a Belgian wholesaler's brand list. A brand carried by a wholesaler frequently has no direct partner programme because the wholesaler is the channel. For many of them, "no transacting programme of their own" is not an observability failure — it is true, and they are not Introw accounts.

Further industrial crawler engineering is not justified. What would change the verdict is a different seed — trade associations, certified-installer registries, manufacturer dealer locators — not a better crawler.

04Distributor inversion: seed source, not backbone

Phase 1 called it the discovery backbone on the strength of: "if a distributor sells your product, you operate a transacting channel." Phase 2 treats that as the hypothesis it is, and it does not survive intact.

  • Cohort B was contaminated by construction. It was seeded from Phase 1's own distributor output, so its 65% carried-rate is evidence about where I sampled, not about plausible targets. Stated plainly because it weakens my own cohort design.
  • The population is disproportionately the wrong side of the relationship — distributed vendors whose reseller relationships are mediated.

What stands from Phase 1: it reliably produces real B2B companies, in local languages, without touching a funding or ATS list. It generates candidates. It does not qualify them.

05Operator resolution — and a detector that was silently dead

channel_operator 40 · unknown 6 · both 2 · distributed_vendor 2 · channel_participant 0
Found by a regression test, not by a run

The participant detector's patterns were anchored on a lowercase we are with no case-insensitive flag. It never fired once across fifty companies. Every earlier "zero participants" observation was a bug, not a finding.

After the fix it was field-tested against real resellers — companies that are participants by definition:

softcat.com        dir=both              participatesIn=Microsoft   ← fires correctly
computacenter.com  dir=channel_operator  participatesIn=none        ← miss
bechtle.com        dir=unknown           participatesIn=none        ← miss
insight.com        dir=unknown           participatesIn=none        ← miss

Field recall is 1 in 4 and owner attribution is noisy — the first run returned "Platinum" and "Microsoft Solution" as vendor names, since fixed by excluding tier words. This is the least-supported component in Phase 2.

The clearest win

Fluke — a manufacturer sold through wholesalers — resolves to distributed_vendor → research_required, not to a qualified operator. That is Phase 1's overstatement corrected in code rather than in prose.

Explicitly not evidence

Company size, employee count, revenue, "enterprise". Factorial has 1,000+ employees and 100+ partner managers and is a customer; Payflip is tiny and is a customer. The red team confirms the model does not use size — Factorial and Axon both land plausible, and Axon is a Salesforce account.

Separating evidence from hypothesis: the distribution discriminator is measured. Incentive complexity and federated governance are hypotheses that failed to fire — defined, implemented, tested, and almost never matching, because the language is not published. They should not be presented as working detectors.

Two rules are enforced in code and covered by tests: found is strong evidence, not found is unknown — never "no PRM"; and Salesforce Experience Cloud is not a PRM, since treating it as decisive produced a false transacting verdict in Phase 1.

A DNS defect worth recording

Under concurrency the dig calls were timing out, the failures were caught, and the empty result was read by the caller as "no record exists" — silently losing the highest-precision detector in the system on companies that plainly have the record. Lookups now return null on failure, distinct from [], and failures are counted and reported.

Competitive displacement is a detectable segment and nothing more. Both readings are returned together and neither is a verdict: maturity — they bought into the category, sized a budget, staffed someone; and switching cost — an incumbent contract with credibility attached. Dissatisfaction, renewal timing and readiness to switch are not observable. An existing platform is structural context, not Why Now.

No layer may answer another layer's question. SAP is the proof: a real transacting channel that is the wrong operating model. Corrupting the classifier to say "not transacting" would have been false — so the demotion lives in a separate dimension.

The card follows the same split. Channel and suitability are adjacent but distinct blocks, and the suitability block carries its reasoning: "Three independent distributors carry this company… This is a demotion on structure — a company one tenth the size with the same structure would read the same."

12Remaining blind spots

  1. The operating model is mostly unpublished. Only the counterparty distribution signal works; the other negative dimensions almost never fire.
  2. The distribution signal is partly a segment proxy — 17% of SaaS-ish companies against 74% of hardware/security/industrial. It still discriminates within distributor-carried companies, but it must never become a hard rule.
  3. Participant detection has thin field validation — 1 in 4 on a small real test.
  4. strong almost never fires. The model demotes; it does not promote.
  5. No timing detector is approved for build. The product can say why an account is interesting and cannot yet say why now.
  6. No people data, no partner count, no operational load at meaningful coverage. Unchanged from Phase 0.
  7. The industrial segment is not served, and the bounded attempt to fix it did not move the number.
  8. The benchmark is 50 companies. Every percentage here has a wide interval.

13Phase 3

Do not build scoring or the GTM Queue yet.

  1. A distribution index as a first-class source. It is the only measured suitability discriminator. Broaden beyond three distributors and treat the vendor–distributor relationship as the primary evidence object.
  2. Our own dated snapshots of partner surfaces and partner-host CNAMEs. Every timing verdict failed for want of a second observation. The single highest-value unlock, and it only needs time to start accruing.
  3. A different industrial seed — trade associations, certified-installer registries, dealer locators — or an explicit decision to scope the product to software and IT.
  4. Programme-level modelling in practice, not just in the schema.
  5. Commercial review as a human surface, before any queue. The model produces a lot of plausible and research_required; that is a review workflow, not a ranked list.

Not recommended: scoring, ranking weights, mass discovery, production UI, CRM detection investment, partner-count engineering.

Measured, not asserted. The suitability benchmark was split before any rule was written and the holdout was run once. Two of my own prior conclusions are corrected here: Phase 1's distributor-inversion claim, and Phase 1's expectation that industrial discovery was mostly fixable plumbing.
