Why this is the most important finding

The earlier precision numbers were partly bought by systematically not seeing the right companies. The system was strongest exactly where Introw is weakest commercially — formalised, mature channel programmes — and blind to the young, informal ones the thesis calls the core ICP.

03Phase 2's discriminator was label leakage

Phase 2's headline was distributor carriage: 1 of 16 customers against 10 of 14 hypothesised poor-fit. The label audit asked how those poor-fit labels were assigned.

The audit result

13 of 14 Cohort C negative rationales cite distribution structure in their own text. I labelled those companies poor-fit because I believed they were distributor-led, then measured that they are distributor-carried. Cohort B was separately sampled from distributor lists, so it inherits the property by construction.

Re-tested against controls whose negative basis is independent — professional-services firms, IT resellers, integration-only SaaS, affiliate programmes:

PopulationnDistributor-carried
Known customers191
Clean negatives140
Matched unlabelled152

Within segment it separates nothing either — software 1/12 customers versus 0/9 unlabelled; security 0/4 versus 1/3; hardware 0/3 versus 1/3. And once ownership is known, carriage adds nothing.

Distribution verdict: unresolved

Stated precisely: this design cannot settle it either way. My clean negatives are firms that never appear on IT-distributor brand lists, so there is no overlap to test. Only 3 of 48 companies are carried at all. Settling it needs clean negatives that are distributor-carried.

In code: the multi-distributor demotion is withdrawn. It now emits research_required with a named research task, not weak.

The commercial conclusion

All four known customers in the band were promotable, and the machine had promoted none. The cost was reading four to six quoted observations — under a minute per account. Evidence capture works; the automatic threshold is conservative. That is the case for a human-in-the-loop product rather than an autonomous one.

Caveats: n=6, from the easier half of the band. Eleven of the seventeen band records carry no positive evidence and would need research from scratch. I am not an Introw AE.

Covered: partner-host DNS/CNAME, platform fingerprints, partner URL inventory, partner surface content. Cadence set by volatility with reasons recorded — DNS weekly, because a CNAME change is the cleanest migration signal and costs nothing.

A correction made mid-flight

My first run reported discovery_seed: SUPPORTED on a count of 72 companies. Inspection showed the extractor was returning title fragments — "Chief Partner", "Strategic Alliances", "April" — and a second bug used a stateful /g regex inside .test(). Both fixed; the verdict is now unresolved. The earlier number should not be cited.

People remains the binding constraint on a GTM queue. No credentials, so Phase 3 delivers a bake-off design rather than a bake-off: a 20–30 company gold set, scored on target-person recall, current-title precision, current-company precision, duplicate rate, role freshness, geography, cost per resolved company and licensing fit.

The 2+ partner-manager rule stays commercially important evidence and is not operationalised as a gate. No compliant source for exact team headcount exists here, and unknown stays neutral: no people found is not team < 2.

12Remaining blind spots

  1. Promotion tracks publication. 6 of 7 rich-density companies promoted, 0 of 21 sparse. Mitigated, not solved.
  2. Automatic promotion is conservative — it misses the informal programmes that are Introw's ICP. A human recovers them; the machine does not.
  3. Materiality has weak recall — 9 of 19. The construct that recovers misses is the one least often observable.
  4. Distribution is unresolved and untestable with these controls.
  5. No people data. Unchanged, and it blocks a queue.
  6. No timing yet. History starts today.
  7. Two bot-blocked customers and one dead domain remain permanently invisible.
  8. n is small throughout — 19 customers, 14 clean negatives, 6 holdout.
  9. Industrial remains not served, frozen from Phase 2.

13What to build, and what not to

Build

  1. Human-in-the-loop review as the product surface. The measurement supports it directly: evidence capture beats the automatic threshold, and a reviewer resolves a record in under a minute.
  2. Keep the snapshot loop running. The only route to Why Now, and it costs nothing but time.
  3. Broaden the materiality probes — the weakest construct, and the one that recovers misses.
  4. A people-provider bake-off as soon as credentials exist. It is the binding constraint on a queue.
  5. The distribution experiment done properly — clean negatives that are themselves distributor-carried.

Do not build

Production scoring · a 0–100 score · a PartnerLedScore · the final GTM Queue · polished production UI · mass discovery · broad industrial crawling · CRM detection investment · partner-count engineering · any Why Now before a second dated observation · the 2+ manager rule as an automatic gate.

Verdict

The system reliably captures the right evidence, quotes it, attributes it, and separates what it proves from what it does not. It demotes clean negatives without error and now promotes a real subset of customer-like programmes. What it cannot yet do is promote autonomously without under-serving the young, informal programmes that are Introw's stated ICP.

Build the human-in-the-loop research Radar. Keep the snapshots accruing. Do not productionise autonomous promotion yet.

Two of my own prior conclusions are corrected here: Phase 2's distribution discriminator is withdrawn as label leakage, and the Phase 1/2 classifier is shown to have been biased against the customers it most needed to find. One Phase 3 result was also corrected mid-flight — an event-source verdict produced by a broken extractor.
