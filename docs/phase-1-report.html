The unseen set is the number that matters. Fifty-six companies produced by the discovery mechanisms, frozen before any classifier ran, carrying no labels at all. Twenty-two were classified transacting: Anomali · A10 · AvePoint · Corelight · Commvault · Deep Instinct · ExtraHop · Fluke · Forcepoint · Gigamon · Illumio · Huawei · NetApp · Nokia · Nutanix · Opengear · Proofpoint · Microsoft · Riverbed · Semperis · Sophos · Rooms. Every one operates a real reseller or distributor channel.

What this establishes

The system is not recognising companies it was tuned on. It found a candidate universe from channel motion — not from funding, not from an ATS, not from a VC portfolio — and classified it correctly on first contact.

A distributor's published vendor list is a near-tautological channel-motion list. If a distributor sells your product, you operate a transacting channel. Three seeds:

exclusive-networks.com/ecosystem/vendors      73 vendors   cybersecurity, EN
infinigate.com/vendors                       106 vendors   IT/security, EN
cebeo.be/catalog/nl-be/brand                 991 brands    electrical industrial, NL

It reaches exactly the segments the thesis calls attractive and structurally invisible. One Belgian electrical wholesaler yields 991 manufacturer brands in Dutch — including Niko, one of my own independently-chosen benchmark picks. Exclusive Networks yields Cubbit, a known Introw customer, discovered from nothing but a distributor's vendor page.

Why the other two under-delivered

Partner-directory harvest failed instructively: the directories worth harvesting are client-rendered, so an HTML link scrape returns nothing. It also answers a slightly wrong question — a vendor's partner directory lists channel participants, and Introw sells to channel operators.

Platform tenancy found 9 tenants on introw.io. Precise, but sparse: most tenants use a custom domain CNAMEd to the vendor, whose certificate is issued under the customer's domain and therefore never appears under the vendor's.

Two design choices do the work, and neither is a weight.

Corroboration. A single strong signal never produces transacting. It produces unknown with the rule single_strong_uncorroborated, which routes the account to Research rather than rejecting it. That is why surfacing (82%) exceeds classification (73%) — those accounts are not lost, they are queued for a human.

Decisive artifacts. A published deal-registration process and a partner subdomain served by a partner platform are decisive alone, because those artifacts do not exist where partners do not transact. 30 of 115 companies were decided this way.

No scoring model

Every verdict returns the named rule that produced it and a sentence a reviewer can disagree with. There are no weights, because there is no conversion data to fit them against — a weighted model here would be false precision wearing a number.

The two artifacts carrying most weight per unit of engineering are PRM_FINGERPRINT — DNS-based, zero false positives — and DEAL_REGISTRATION, a process that cannot exist without a channel.

PARTNER_PORTAL is the most common strong class but the weakest of the strong ones: a portal proves a gated surface exists, not that anything transacts through it. It never decides a verdict alone.

Unplanned finding

Four competitor platform fingerprints turned up in the unseen set — Allbound at ExtraHop and Semperis, Impartner at Nokia and Proofpoint. PRM displacement is a detectable segment, not a nurture hope.

One case deliberately not "fixed"

SAP operates one of the largest reseller channels in existence, so classifying it transacting is correct. It is nonetheless a poor Introw account, because multi-tier distributor governance sits outside Introw's design. That is a prioritisation problem, so the demotion lives in a separate multiTierSuspected dimension — 14 companies carry it — rather than in the classifier. Tuning the classifier to call SAP "not transacting" would have been false, and would have been benchmark-memorisation.

The industrial gap decomposes into three causes, and only one is about channel evidence:

  1. Entity resolution (≈8 of 21). Parked domains, wrong entities, ampersand names. Plumbing.
  2. Locale-gated and very large catalogue sites (≈5). Thousands of URLs, none reachable from the homepage.
  3. Genuine non-publication (≈8). Small manufacturers with no public partner surface at all.

Only the third is a real observability limit. The first two are engineering, and that is where the next effort belongs.

An industrial-specific trap the software segment never had

These sites use channel words as product nouns: /prodotti/.../distributori-pneumatici/ is pneumatic distributors, not distribution. Product paths are now excluded — a rule that only exists because the industrial segment was measured.

07Partner counts — 2%, and that is the honest number

unknown          90     the majority state
lower_bound      23     partial enumeration, pagination not exhausted
directory_count   1     cumulocity.com — 78 partners, from the PartnerPage.io payload
exact_public      1     teamleader.eu — 438 partners, stated by the company

The single directory count is worth dwelling on. partnerlisting.corp.cumulocity.com was found by passive DNS, ranked only after the ranker learned to read hostnames as well as paths, and counted by parsing a Nuxt payload. Three separate mechanisms had to work for one number — which is exactly why the rate is 2%.

The count type is part of the value. A lower bound, an approximate figure and an enumerated directory must never render identically: Phase 0 measured a 22% gap between Cumulocity's public directory (78) and the company's own stated figure ("100+").

08Domain model

Two rules govern every type.

A value is never stored bare. Fact<T> carries value, evidence state, confidence, the method in words, sources, observation date, and what it superseded. EvidenceState is confirmed · strong_proxy · weak_proxy · contradicted · unknown · blocked — and blocked is distinct from unknown, because "we could not look" is not "there is nothing there".

unknown is a value, not a gap. CompanyIdentity carries an identityState because resolution is a measured error source. PartnerCount carries a countType and whether enumeration completed. OperationalLoad carries an availability and the reason it is unavailable. Signal keeps effectiveAt separate from observedAt, firstSeenAt and lastSeenAt.

RelationshipState has no not_customer member. Absence of an Introw fingerprint is no_evidence_observed, and nothing more.

09UX contract — designed for the sparse account

Fourteen fixtures, each proving one state renders honestly. The card has no fixed metric slots; it leads with whatever is strongest and gives unknown states equal visual weight.

  • Partner count unknown reads "Not established — no countable directory found; this is not a claim that the network is small", not an empty box.
  • Team size unknown reads "No partner owner identified. Company-owned pages do not normally name this role."
  • Operational load appears only when both inputs are independently reliable — 1 of 14 fixtures. Otherwise it is absent, with the reason in the drawer.
  • Suppressed accounts stay visible with the reason stated. A suppression the seller cannot see is indistinguishable from a bug.

The fixture header reports its own sparseness — 8 of 14 channel-confirmed, 8 of 14 CRM unknown, 11 of 14 team size unknown, 1 of 14 with a computable operational load. That is roughly the real distribution, and designing against it is the point.

A new product state Phase 1 forced

single_strong_uncorroborated is neither qualified nor rejected, and it needs its own treatment: a Research candidate with the one signal shown and the missing corroboration named. Without it, two of twenty-two known customers would silently vanish.

10Detector roadmap, by measured value

  1. Distributor-inversion harvester — the discovery backbone. Add EU wholesalers per segment (HVAC, plumbing, solar, industrial automation) to reach the industrial market in its own languages.
  2. Entity resolution v2 — the highest-leverage fix available. Currently the largest cause of industrial false negatives, ahead of any evidence problem.
  3. PRM/platform CNAME fingerprinter — highest precision per unit of effort; already yielding competitor displacement targets.
  4. Locale-aware crawler — partly built; large catalogue sites still defeat it.
  5. Vendor-directory payload parsers — the count problem is otherwise unsolvable at scale.
  6. Common Crawl history backfill — turns a static count into a dated change, the only honest route to "Why Now".
  7. Carve-out / M&A watch — still Unresolved. No feed tested in either phase, and it remains the highest-conviction trigger with no measured observability basis.
  8. CRM detector — maintain, do not invest.

11Recommendation

Segmented Go

Proceed for software, hardware and IT/security vendors. Discovery produces a genuine candidate universe from channel motion rather than from financing. Classification separates real channels from the dangerous traps at 100% trap precision and 22/22 apparent precision on unseen data. The evidence is defensible to a seller, because every verdict carries its rule and its sources.

Do not yet claim the industrial segment. It classifies at 5% against 60–69% for software. The cause is mostly fixable plumbing, but it is not fixed today, and the honest position is that the radar currently sees the industrial market poorly. That matters, because it is the segment where the thesis argues Introw is least contested.

What would move this to a full GO

Entity resolution good enough that a Belgian wholesaler's brand list becomes a usable account list, and a locale-aware crawler that reaches partner pages on multi-country catalogue sites. Both are bounded engineering against a measurable target — the same 21 industrial companies, already frozen.

What would move it to NO-GO

If resolution and locale crawling were fixed and the industrial segment still classified below roughly 30%, the conclusion would be that these companies genuinely do not publish channel evidence — and the product should be scoped to software and IT explicitly, rather than quietly under-serving everything else.

Measured, not asserted. Frozen sets were not edited after results were seen. The one benchmark label I believe is wrong — Anthropic, labelled a small-strategic-alliances trap, which publishes referral-partner programme terms — is reported here rather than corrected in the frozen file.
