What the Radar can actually see

The commercial thesis is largely right about what makes an account interesting, and substantially wrong about what is observable. Three of its four hard gates cannot be operated as gates. Here is the evidence, and the model that survives it.

00How this was answered

Phase 0 asked whether the intelligence the Radar wants to display can actually be discovered, dated, resolved and verified reliably enough for production.

I did not answer it by reasoning about the thesis. I froze a 69-company benchmark before running anything, built the detectors, and ran them. Every number here comes from a real request made on 21 Aug 2026. Where a source was not tested, it says so rather than estimating.

The benchmark has four cohorts: 22 known Introw customers (fit reconstruction), 20 likely-fit non-customers chosen by independent structural reasoning (generalisation), 17 controls and traps (precision), and a 10-company persona gold set. Customer status and trap class are ground truth. Everything else — segment labels, domain mappings, expected verdicts — is human judgment, and Cohort B carries no labels at all by design.

Known leakage, stated up front

Cohort A companies are live Introw customers now. Their current portal and PRM fingerprint are plausibly Introw's own product. Cohort A therefore measures discoverability, classification and customer suppression — never timing. Reading a portal detection on Cohort A as a prospecting result would be circular.

A1What survived, and got stronger

Transacting partners ≠ integration partners — and the distinction is detectableUpgraded

The thesis calls integration-ecosystem presence "the single most dangerous buildable signal." Measured: 4 of 4 integration-only controls — Linear, Sentry, PostHog, Vercel — were correctly refused a transacting verdict, while 18 of 19 reachable known customers exposed a partner surface. The load-bearing distinction in the whole model holds empirically. It moves from reasoned inference to measured.

PRM fingerprinting is far stronger than the thesis assumed — and it runs on DNSUpgraded

The thesis rates PRM fingerprinting "moderate" observability via login-page artifacts. The real mechanism is DNS: partners.<domain> CNAMEs straight to the vendor. Measured on Cohort A: 9 of 19 reachable known customers (47%) resolve to cname.introw.io, with zero false positives anywhere in the 69-company benchmark.

Two consequences the thesis does not draw. First, this generalises to every competitor with a hosted portal, which turns PRM displacement from a nurture hope into a detectable segment. Second, and this is a release gate: it is an existing-customer suppression detector. Nine accounts in a 69-company benchmark are current Introw customers. At scale, cold-mailing your own customers is the most embarrassing failure this system can produce, and this is the cheapest guard against it.

A public partner surface is close to proof a channel existsHeld

No control company lacking a channel produced a transacting verdict on a real partner page.

A2What failed, and must change before implementation

Four load-bearing specification items did not survive contact with measurement. Each is written correctly in the thesis's prose and wrongly in its radar specification.

1. The CRM gate cannot operate as a hard gateFailed

The thesis calls HubSpot "the hardest qualification gate in the model — harder than industry, geography, or size," and specifies it as gate_1 at CONFIRMED evidence standard. Measured, sweeping up to ten form-bearing pages per company:

And on the four customers whose HubSpot use Introw itself publishes: Cumulocity — not detected. Factorial — not detected, across nine inspected pages. Factorial's own application config carries showHubspotChatModal flags — their code references HubSpot — but no live artifact is served.

Consequence

Applied as specified, this gate discards roughly four-fifths of a realistic prospect universe on absence of evidence — and discards two of Introw's own flagship references. That directly violates the thesis's own invariant: absence of evidence is never scored as negative evidence.

2. Salesforce accounts are not a secondary ICP — one of them is a customerContradicted

partners.axon.com CNAMEs to Introw. Axon's own distributor page serves go.pardot.com — Salesforce. No HubSpot artifact appears anywhere on the site. Independently re-verified outside the cache.

The thesis's "4/4 disclosed stacks are HubSpot" is an artifact of which case studies Introw chose to publish — exactly as its own bias section warned. HubSpot is the dominant pattern, not a binary gate. And because Salesforce is the less detectable of the two, a hard gate compounds a marketing-selection bias with an observability bias pointing the same way.

3. The two-partner-manager floor is the model's most expensive gate, not its cheapestFailed on observability

The thesis: "a single LinkedIn title count — the least ambiguous, fastest, lowest-cost qualification step in the entire model," to be run first, before any crawl.

Measured. LinkedIn company people pages return a login wall; profile URLs return HTTP 999. Both are also ToS-constrained. The free alternatives, against a ten-company gold set:

  • Company team / about / leadership pages: 1 named partner-persona person across 10 companies. Sedai's about page lists seven executives — CEO, CTO, CRO, VP Marketing, SVP Engineering, VP Product, VP Engineering — and no partner leader, while Introw's own case study names Sedai's Head of Partners & Alliances. The partner leader sits below the published executive cut, systematically.
  • Company partner pages: 0 named partner-persona people across 10 companies.

The denominator of the model's primary scoring variable is not observable from free public sources. This is the BOND person-discovery failure repeating in a new form — caught this time before discovery rather than after.

4. Partner-leverage ratio cannot be the primary scoring variableFailed

Numerator. A partner count was directly countable for 5 of 61 reachable companies by explicit stated count, 24 by any method, and 37 not at all. Where it is countable it is a lower bound, not a census — Cumulocity's public directory holds 78 entries (extracted from its PartnerPage.io payload) against a company-stated "100+", a ~22% undercount.

Denominator. Unavailable, per finding 3.

And the threshold is contradicted by Introw's own evidence. The specification calls ≥ 20 strong. Factorial — 500+ partners, 100+ partnership managers — is a ratio of ~5. Introw's flagship enterprise reference sits in the specification's "weak" band. The ratio is an excellent description of the pain. It is not a computable primary score.

A3Changed in degree

Partnership events: right about precision, wrong about populationWeakened

The thesis promotes event attendance to Tier 1 and argues the public subset — exhibitors, sponsors, speakers — "is the higher-intent subset anyway." Measured across eight partnership and channel organisers: 233 people extracted, 70 by clean structured methods, of which 21 (30%) are Tier-1 partner leadership with usable titles. Precision is genuinely high.

But 26% of the corpus references large-cap employers — Microsoft, AWS, SAP, Oracle, IBM, Salesforce, BCG, EY, NTT DATA, Mastercard, ServiceNow, Snowflake. Introw's G2 enterprise share is 1.9%.

The refinement that matters

Introw buys $50k booths to meet mid-market partner managers in the aisles. Those people are attendees, and attendee lists are private. The published part of the event population is the part Introw does not sell to. The signal is real; its observable slice is systematically the wrong size band. Tier 1 for person discovery — not for account prioritisation.

Still unresolved

  • Carve-out / MBO detection. The thesis's highest-conviction trigger, with two independent customer confirmations. No M&A feed or company registry was tested this session. It is the one Tier-1 trigger with no measured observability basis. Unresolved
  • The two-manager floor itself. Founder-stated with churn evidence behind it; I have no way to test it externally and no reason to doubt it. It is the measurement that failed, not the rule.
  • The ~400 unconverted free-tier installs. Untestable without access. The thesis calls this the highest-leverage action available and I agree — nothing I built beats it.

BThe qualification model that survives

Five sequential hard gates become one hard gate, a suppression pass, and four eligibility states. The change is forced: only one gate can be evaluated reliably enough to exclude on.

The single hard gate

GATE  transacting_channel_evidence == present
      satisfied by ANY of:
        · a partner subdomain with a vendor-attributable CNAME   (strongest)
        · a partner page classified transacting on main content
        · a deal-registration or partner-login surface
        · a countable partner / dealer / installer directory
      AND NOT matched by an active suppression rule

The one structural change to make

The thesis orders gates cheapest-first and fails fast. Measurement inverts the cost order. The correct order is evidence-density-first: run the cheap, high-precision, high-availability checks — DNS, certificate transparency, Common Crawl, site crawl — across the whole universe, and spend the scarce expensive resource, human research and any licensed people-data credits, only on accounts that already cleared the channel gate. Gate 0 moves from first to last, and stops being a gate at all.

Blocking finding

For the company-first question the product is built around — "who should an Introw seller contact at this account?" — there is no viable free public source for the Tier-1 persona. Company team pages yielded one named partner person across ten companies. Partner pages yielded none. LinkedIn is technically blocked and ToS-constrained. Sanctioned web search resolved one attempt in four.

This forces a product decision rather than an engineering one. Three options, not mutually exclusive:

  1. Procure a licensed people-data provider — Proxycurl, People Data Labs, Apollo, Cognism or equivalent. Needs credentials and budget. Untested; I hold no credentials.
  2. Run person-first as a parallel lane. Event and community corpora give name + title + employer with no company-first lookup at all. That population is enterprise-skewed, so it seeds some accounts well and most badly.
  3. Model person: unknown as a first-class state that routes to Research and blocks GTM-Ready without blocking the account.

Option 3 is mandatory regardless. Without option 1, the majority of qualified accounts reach the queue with no named contact, and the product's honest answer to "who do I call?" is "we don't know yet — here is the research task."

FSource architecture

Three tiers, ordered by what the measurements justify.

Tier 1 — build now, all validated this session

DNS CNAME resolution with wildcard control · passive DNS host enumeration · certificate transparency · site crawl driven by sitemap and link graph · Common Crawl CDX for historical backfill and cheap per-domain screening.

Passive DNS and certificate transparency are complementary, not redundant. Each found partner surfaces the other missed, and both found surfaces that path probing and crawling could not:

found only by passive DNS  partnerprogram.niko.eu · channel.teamleader.eu
                          partnerhub.corp.cumulocity.com
                          partner-academy.vanta.com

found only by cert transparency  partners.quatt.io · deal-api.quatt.io

found only by crawling  loxone.com — 13 partner surfaces,
                          no partner subdomain at all
The case that settles the design

Cumulocity returns zero partner surfaces by crawling and decisive evidence by DNS. The best-evidenced customer in the entire thesis is invisible to a crawl-only architecture.

Tier 2 — after approval

Partnership event and community corpora (the person-first lane) · vendor-hosted directory payload parsers, PartnerPage.io first · sanctioned web search as a research aid, never a scoring input.

Tier 3 — requires a decision, not engineering

Licensed people-data provider · Introw's own CRM and free-tier install base · M&A and carve-out feeds · company registries.

Rejected on evidence

LinkedIn scraping — blocked and ToS-constrained. SERP scraping — CAPTCHA on the second call. PRM vendor logo pages — thin, four of ten returned 404. Common Crawl as a discovery source — the index is domain-keyed, so you cannot ask "which hosts have a /become-a-partner page."

GDetector requirements

Each of these exists because a measurement demanded it.

  1. Wildcard-controlled subdomain resolver. Probe a random subdomain first; treat only targets differing from the wildcard as evidence. Without it, 53% of hits are noise.
  2. PRM/vendor CNAME fingerprinter, with an Introw branch that suppresses rather than scores.
  3. Non-production host filter. dev.partnerprogram.niko.eu exists alongside the real one. A staging portal is not a portal.
  4. Main-content extraction before lexicon classification. Ringover's genuine reseller programme classified integration_only purely because its mega-nav advertises "100+ integrations". Site chrome defeats whole-page classification.
  5. Partner-page ranker that demotes integration pages. Factorial's top-ranked partner URL was /integrations-partner-program; its real one was /partnerships.
  6. Directory counter with declared method and a lower-bound label. Never presented as a census; always carries method and observedAt.
  7. Vendor-directory payload parsers — PartnerPage.io Nuxt payload extraction yielded count: 78 on Cumulocity where static HTML counting found nothing.
  8. Persona classifier with anti-personas resolving first, verified in both directions: "HR Business Partner", "Managing Partner, Tax" and "Marketplace Manager" correctly refused; "Global Head, Alliance Ecosystem and Programs" and "Director, Global Partner Programs & Operations" correctly accepted.
  9. Person-record boundary detection. Flat-text extraction glues adjacent speakers together. Only structured extraction — JSON-LD, heading-plus-sibling — produces records trustworthy enough for GTM Ready.
  10. Common Crawl history backfiller, keyed per domain and unioned across collections. Single collections are volatile: Enphase returned 7 captures in one and 800 in another.
  11. Bot-block classifier that records bot_protection distinctly from not_found. "We could not look" is not "nothing is there."

The invariant this enforces

The majority state for most fields on most accounts is unknown. A design that treats unknown as a gap to be filled will fill it with proxies. The Radar must treat unknown as information — it is precisely what tells a seller which question to ask first.

I / JFailure classes, ranked

False positives

  1. Professional-services firms with real alliance ecosystems. Deloitte returned transacting across 692 partner surfaces. Equity-partner suppression and alliance detection fire simultaneously and the wrong one wins. Suppress on firm type, not programme lexicon.
  2. Enterprise multi-tier channel read as fit. SAP (2,692 partner surfaces), Schneider, Cisco all classify transacting — correctly. The failure is treating a correct classification as a qualification. Needs an explicit demotion, not a better classifier.
  3. DNS wildcards read as portals. 53% of naive hits. Mitigated by the control probe.
  4. Staging hosts read as production surfaces.
  5. Site chrome read as programme content.
  6. Agency and affiliate programmes read as reseller channels. Kinsta returned transacting on a 300-agency programme — genuinely ambiguous. Route to Research; do not auto-resolve.
  7. Entity mis-resolution. Identity must be verified before its evidence is trusted.
  8. Glued person records from flat-text extraction.

False negatives

  1. Channels living only on a non-guessable subdomain. Cumulocity is invisible to crawling. Mitigated by passive DNS + CT.
  2. Bot-protected companies (~10%). No mitigation available. Must be recorded as blocked, never as "no channel".
  3. Salesforce accounts. 0% detection on Cohort B — and a confirmed customer is one of them.
  4. EU industrials and local-language mid-market. Loxone, Duco, Niko, Yuki all have real channels. None produced a CRM artifact, none appear in the event corpora, none are findable by the person sources. The thesis calls this segment commercially attractive precisely because competitors miss it — and this radar misses it too, everywhere except channel existence.
  5. Login-walled portals. partners.quatt.io proves a portal exists and reveals nothing about its size.
  6. Client-rendered directories without a vendor parser.
  7. Companies below the published-content threshold — bootstrapped, non-VC, small footprint.

KArchitecture recommendation

Do not inherit BOND's shape. Inherit its epistemics.

Keep: evidence provenance with retrievedAt per fact · source authority tiers · verified-facts precedence · the commercial-review layer between model and queue · queue blockers as code · identity resolved before evidence is trusted · raw-universe immutability · graceful degradation · scores re-derived at load · validation as an exit-code gate.

Reject: the YC/Getro discovery universe — it structurally cannot see industrials, bootstrapped companies or EU local-language companies, four of Introw's six segments · person discovery attached to company discovery, the exact failure that produced 648 founders · additive 0–100 scoring · BOND's signal taxonomy, personas and weights.

The five changes Phase 0 forces

  1. Company-first and person-first are separate lanes with separate sources, joined by identity resolution — not one pipeline where people fall out of company discovery. This is the single most important decision here, and it is the direct lesson of the BOND post-mortem.
  2. resolveFact() as a real function, not a coalesce order. Every field carries {value, evidenceState, method, sourceUrl, observedAt, retrievedAt, confidence, supersedes} and precedence becomes structural rather than procedural.
  3. Snapshot store from day one. Change claims are the product's "Why Now". firstSeenAt ≠ effectiveAt ≠ observedAt ≠ retrievedAt — and Common Crawl backfill means history exists at launch rather than in six months.
  4. Research is a first-class object, not a fallback. A large share of qualified accounts will legitimately be "channel confirmed, person unknown". That is a product state, and the queue must represent it without either lying or hiding the account.
  5. Storage: file-based JSON is adequate at benchmark scale and will not survive scheduled re-verification with per-field freshness across thousands of accounts. Build the domain model against a repository interface now; swap the implementation when scale demands it. Do not build the database yet.

NGo / No-Go

Conditional GO for Phase 1, on the revised model, with two blocking questions.

The commercial thesis is sound about what makes an account interesting. It is wrong about what can be seen. Rebuilding the qualification model around that — one hard gate, states instead of gates, unknown as a first-class value — produces a system that is smaller than the mockups promise and defensible in front of a CRO. Building the specified model instead produces a system that looks complete and is quietly wrong in four places.

What I recommend proceeding with: the Phase 1 domain model and fixture UX, designed around the sparse account rather than the full one; then the Tier-1 detectors, which are all validated.

Blocking question 1 Do we buy licensed people data, or design around unknown?

Without a provider — Proxycurl, People Data Labs, Apollo, Cognism or equivalent — the product cannot answer "who do I contact at this account" for most accounts, and neither the partner-team gate nor the operational-load metric can be populated at all. I hold no credentials and have tested none of them. Both paths are buildable; they produce materially different products.

Blocking question 2 Is Introw's own CRM and free-tier install base coming?

The thesis's own conclusion is that cross-referencing the ~400 unconverted installs against the two-manager floor outranks any external radar for the first quarter of outbound. Nothing I built this session beats it. If that access is coming, it should shape Phase 1. If it is not, I should stop designing for it.

Neither blocks starting Phase 1. Both change what Phase 1 should optimise for — so I would rather ask once, now, than assume.
